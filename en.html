WHY UNDERVOLT?

More performance.
At any cost?

For years, GPU tuning mostly meant higher clocks, more voltage and a few extra FPS. Higher power consumption, more heat and louder fans were often accepted as the price.

But does more always make sense? Undervolting focuses on efficiency: finding the right voltage for the performance you want. Using less energy can also mean less heat and lower temperatures.

Your graphics card was expensive. Treat it well.
Get the best out of it with lower power consumption in mind. Play helps you find the balance between performance, temperature and power draw.

Savings and performance depend on your card, game and settings. Test your profile on your own system.

Getting started & checking results

01 / AN EASY START

I want to start undervolting.
What do I need?

Getting started is simple: open Play and click one of the prepared UV profiles. This activates undervolting for your card. Play applies the saved settings for you.

You need a supported GeForce RTX desktop card, Windows 11 and Play. You do not have to create a curve or enter voltage values by hand to take your first step.

  1. 01 Click a UV profile
  2. 02 Launch a game
  3. 03 Compare readings

Launch one of your games and use telemetry to watch clock, voltage, power draw and FPS. See what the profile does on your card, and check that your game remains stable.

02 / NV-UV OR NV-UV PLAY?

I heard NV-UV needs Afterburner.
Does that apply to Play?

It depends on the app: NV-UV is the companion app for MSI Afterburner. Those two work together.

NV-UV Play is fully standalone. Play does not need Afterburner. Undervolting, profile management and telemetry are built directly into Play.

To get started with Play, you only need Play itself. You do not need to follow the instructions for the NV-UV companion app.

03 / COMMUNITY PROFILES · UV TRY

Found settings in a forum or on social media.
Now follow a tutorial?

The voltage and clock look promising. But before you follow a tutorial step by step, questions remain:

  • Which card were these settings for?
  • Where do I enter the values? Do I need to build a curve?
  • Will this be stable on my card? What do others say?

Already have specific settings from a forum or video? Enter them directly in Play: open UV Curve, select the suggested voltage and enter the clock. One click on Apply curve applies and saves the UV curve. You do not have to build it point by point. Test these settings on your own card too.

Prefer to choose an existing profile? Use profiles from the NV-UV community. UV Try preselects your detected GPU model. Compare profiles and choose a suitable starting point.

Import a profile into a slot, test it in your games and rate your experience. You do not have to create a curve by hand. The community helps itself, and the next person can benefit from your experience.

  1. 01 Choose a profile
  2. 02 Test on your GPU
  3. 03 Rate your experience

Results can differ even with the same GPU model. Ratings help you choose; stability still needs to be checked on your card.

04 / TELEMETRY & OVERLAY

How can I tell whether my GPU is undervolted?

You activated a profile, but what actually changed? The readings help you find out. Use Play’s built-in telemetry and show the values in its overlay while gaming. Alternatively, monitor them with HWiNFO or GPU-Z.

Clock · MHz
What clock is your GPU running under load?
Voltage · mV / V
What voltage is it using? For example, 0.950 V equals 950 mV.
Power draw · Watts
How much power is the GPU using? Keep an eye on FPS as well.

Compare the same game scene with identical graphics settings and FPS cap, once at stock and once with your UV profile. Lower voltage at a comparable clock shows the effect of undervolting. Lower watts at a similar frame rate makes the efficiency gain visible.

Low power draw alone is not proof: a lighter load also lowers readings. Idle readings therefore tell you little about your profile.

05 / STABILIZER

Undervolting.
Does it always have to be trial and error?

A profile works well until a game crashes the driver. Adjust clocks and voltage, then test again. Stabilizer takes over some of that follow-up work.

Play adjusts after a detected driver crash. Depending on the strategy, it lowers the clock, raises voltage or combines both. This works with game profiles and the global profile, within your configured limits.

Automation while gaming

06 / UV PILOT

My games have different requirements.
Do I have to switch every time?

An older title runs smoothly with a power-saving profile. A demanding game needs more performance, and you have tuned a custom UV curve for your favorite title. One profile does not always suit everything.

UV Pilot handles profile switching. Tailor your UV profiles to each game and assign them in the Game Library. When UV Pilot detects a game, Play automatically uses its assigned profile.

Find your balance between performance, consumption and temperature for each game, without switching manually before every launch.

07 / DCC

FPS capped.
Why is the GPU clock still so high?

You set a frame cap, but the card still runs at a high clock and correspondingly high voltage. Is that really necessary for your target frame rate?

This is where DCC comes in. DCC Automatic gradually finds a suitable in-game clock, taking performance and utilization into account. Less unnecessary clock speed can mean lower power draw and less heat.

DCC and Stabilizer are used alternatively. Results depend on your system; not every crash is detected, and stability still needs to be checked in your games.

For experienced users

08 / FINE-TUNING

I already know undervolting.
What does Play offer me?

Quite a lot. Adjust your own UV curve, use experimental advanced voltage settings or test your GPU with the Voltage Step Scanner. Check specific voltage points and explore suitable settings for your card.

Want to begin with a conservative profile for your own card? Auto-UV using the NVIDIA method derives a starting point from its factory curve. Save it to a profile slot and then test it in your games.

09 / FAN CONTROL

Can I adjust cooling and noise myself?

Want your GPU to stay cool while gaming without being unnecessarily loud? Open Fan curve directly in the Play main window. Choose GPU automatic control, a fixed fan setting or your own fan curve based on temperature.

This lets you adjust cooling and noise to suit your everyday use. Apply & save applies and saves your settings.

Fan control is still being improved. Zero Fan, meaning a complete fan stop, does not work yet.

Questions before you start?

Which graphics cards does Play support?

GeForce RTX desktop models in the RTX 20, RTX 30, RTX 40 and RTX 50 series on Windows 11. Individual features depend on the GPU and driver.

Are notebooks supported?

Not yet. Play is currently intended for desktop graphics cards.

Does Play work with a multi-GPU setup?

Do you have more than one GeForce graphics card in your PC? Yes, Play lets you select a target GPU. Under Settings → NVIDIA GPU for Play, choose the card you want Play to control. Save the selection and restart Play.

Profiles, readings and DCC Automatic use the selected card. The limitation applies to the separate DCC NVIDIA Experimental mode (Power Efficiency Mode), which currently requires a single NVIDIA GPU. The Voltage Step Scanner has the same restriction.

Selecting a GPU in Play does not change the GPU used by a game or Lossless Scaling. Full multi-GPU hardware validation is still pending.

Do I need MSI Afterburner for Play?

No. NV-UV is the companion app for MSI Afterburner. NV-UV Play is a standalone application and does not require Afterburner. Use Play separately from MSI Afterburner for GPU tuning.

Does Play cost anything?

No. NV-UV Play is free of charge.

Is Play open source?

NV-UV Play is currently not open source. Please refer to the included license notices for terms of use and further licensing questions.

What should I know about the alpha?

The developer has tested the alpha on RTX 50. Other GPU families need further validation; RTX 20 and RTX 30 are experimental. Multi-GPU hardware tests are still pending. New hardware writes, such as experimental voltage settings and fan control, have not been comprehensively validated.

The overlay supports desktop, windowed and borderless modes. Exclusive fullscreen has not been supported since v2.0.8.

Do I need to test profiles on my card?

Yes. Community-tested profiles and Auto-UV provide a starting point. Every GPU and game can behave differently. Test settings on your system and follow the notices in Play. A passed test and Stabilizer do not guarantee lasting stability.
